Featured Project · Data Analysis

Mathematics — Statistics Data Analysis Project

Oct – Nov 2025 Ateneo de Manila University Team Leader
01

Project Overview

This project examined the relationship between social media usage, mental health, and overall well-being across different demographic groups using a real-world dataset sourced from Kaggle.

Rather than focusing only on statistical calculations, the project involved selecting appropriate analytical methods, data cleaning, interpreting relationships within the data, and translating the results into evidence-based conclusions. The analysis considered behavioral factors such as screen time, sleep quality, stress, and exercise, as well as differences across age groups and social media platforms.

Project analysis and statistical work
02

My Role

As Team Leader, I helped organize the team's analytical workflow and guided the development and presentation of the project. My responsibilities included:

  • Coordinating the team's tasks and ensuring that project components were completed and integrated.
  • Helping analyze and interpret the dataset using the appropriate statistical methods.
  • Reviewing the results to identify meaningful patterns and relationships within the data.
  • Contributing to the development of graphs and other visual representations of the findings.
  • Helping translate the statistical results into clear, evidence-based conclusions for the final presentation.
03

Research Question & Subquestions

Main Question
How does social media usage influence the mental health and overall well-being of individuals across different demographic groups?

The project examined this through four subquestions:

  • Screen Time, Stress, and Happiness — How does daily screen time correlate with stress level and happiness index?
  • Social Media Platform — How does platform type, such as Facebook, Instagram, or TikTok, affect stress and happiness?
  • Sleep Quality and Stress — What is the relationship between sleep quality and stress level?
  • Happiness and Multiple Factors — Can we predict happiness level using demographic and behavioral variables such as age, gender, screen time, stress, and sleep quality?
04

Statistical & Analytical Tools

The analysis used several statistical methods to examine relationships and differences within the dataset:

  • Pearson Correlation — measured the direction and strength of linear relationships between variables such as screen time, stress, sleep quality, and happiness.
  • One-Way ANOVA — examined whether stress and happiness differed significantly across social media platform groups.
  • Graph Theory & Graph Coloring — used as a visual and analytical framework for representing relationships, grouping characteristics, and comparing indicators across respondents or age groups.
  • Data Visualization — translated statistical results into graphs and visual representations to make patterns across the dataset easier to interpret.

Social Media Platform

The One-Way ANOVA results did not show statistically significant differences in either stress (p = 0.342) or happiness (p = 0.127) across the social media platform groups examined.

While some visual differences appeared between platform groups, these differences were not statistically significant at the 0.05 level.

Sleep Quality and Stress

Sleep quality had a moderate negative association with stress (r = −0.58).

This indicates that higher sleep-quality scores tended to correspond with lower stress scores within the dataset.

Multiple Factors and Happiness

The correlation analysis showed that stress had the strongest observed relationship with happiness (r = −0.737), followed by screen time (r = −0.705) and sleep quality (r = 0.679). Exercise showed a very weak relationship with happiness (r = 0.041).

Key Finding

Information Systems Consulting Case Study

Nov 2025 Ateneo de Manila University Project Lead
01

Project Overview

This consulting-style case study involved developing an information systems proposal for Endymion Amarillo College, a simulated institution seeking to upgrade its facilities for HyFlex learning and VR/AR-enabled instruction.

The challenge was not simply to recommend new technology, but to determine how different systems, infrastructure, and resources could work together to address the organization's operational and instructional needs.

The proposal covered the college's hardware, software, network infrastructure, data storage, security, training, maintenance, and implementation requirements. The team evaluated the organization's existing setup, identified gaps, and designed an integrated solution intended to balance functionality, user needs, scalability, security, and cost-efficiency.

My Role

Question

Central Case Question
How can Endymion Amarillo College upgrade its facilities and information systems to support HyFlex learning and VR/AR-enabled instruction while addressing connectivity, infrastructure, security, and operational needs?

The case required the team to work backward from the organization's needs rather than starting with individual products. The central consideration was:

For example, the college's need for reliable HyFlex instruction led to requirements for audio/video capture, classroom displays, network capacity, software, storage, and connectivity. These components were then designed to work together rather than treated as separate purchases.

HyFlex logical system diagram
04

Systems Analysis & Solution Design

The proposal translated the organization's requirements into several interconnected areas:

Hardware Infrastructure

Evaluated existing equipment and proposed upgrades or new equipment based on the requirements of HyFlex classrooms, laboratories, and VR/AR activities.

Software Systems

Recommended platforms for virtual instruction, classroom management, digital design, and VR workflows, while considering existing systems and licensing needs.

Network Infrastructure

Designed a Wi-Fi 6 network based on estimated device usage and bandwidth requirements, including access-point placement, VLAN segmentation, and Quality of Service prioritization.

Data & Security

Proposed a hybrid storage environment using on-premises NAS and cloud storage, together with security monitoring and controlled access.

Implementation & Integration

Considered how the different technologies would be connected through mechanisms such as Single Sign-On, cloud storage, network infrastructure, and existing institutional systems.

Training & Transition

Included training, documentation, maintenance, and knowledge-transfer plans so the organization could operate and sustain the proposed system after implementation.

This approach allowed the proposal to address the organization as a system of connected requirements, rather than treating each technology decision independently. For instance, network capacity was planned around the expected number and type of devices, while software licensing and VR infrastructure were considered alongside actual instructional use and cost optimization.

Network logical architecture
05

From Requirements to Solution

One of the central parts of the project was connecting organizational requirements to specific design decisions.

Support simultaneous in-person and remote learning
HyFlex audio/video equipment and integrated classroom setup
Maintain reliable connectivity under high device loads
Wi-Fi 6 infrastructure, access-point capacity planning, VLANs and QoS
Support VR/AR activities for Interior Design students
VR headsets, upgraded workstations, and design/VR software
Manage recordings and learning materials
Hybrid NAS + cloud storage architecture
Protect institutional and student data
Role-based access, encryption, monitoring, and security controls
Ensure long-term adoption
Faculty/IT training, documentation, maintenance, and transition plans

The proposal therefore considered not only “What technology should be purchased?”, but also “Why is it needed, how will it interact with the existing environment, who will use it, and how will the organization maintain it?”

06

Key Systems Thinking Takeaway

The project demonstrated an information-systems approach to problem solving: technology decisions were derived from organizational requirements and considered in relation to people, processes, infrastructure, data, and long-term operations.

Rather than proposing a collection of standalone technologies, the team developed a connected solution that considered integration, resource utilization, security, training, maintenance, and transition to operations alongside the initial technology requirements.

Systems Thinking Principle

The system was treated as something the organization would need to operate and sustain — not simply purchase and install.
